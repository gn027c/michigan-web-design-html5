Top Natural Destinations in Michigan

Michigan is surrounded by four of the five Great Lakes, boasting over 3,000 miles of freshwater coastline. Whether traveling to the Upper Peninsula or along Lake Michigan, travelers find scenic views, sandy dunes, and untouched wilderness.

Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore

Rising high above Lake Michigan, the towering golden sand dunes offer panoramic views of turquoise water and the Manitou Islands. Hiking the Dune Climb is a favorite tradition for visitors of all ages.

Tahquamenon Falls State Park

Famous for the Upper Falls—one of the largest waterfalls east of the Mississippi River—this wilderness park stretches across acres of peaceful northern hardwood forest in the Upper Peninsula.

Top Seasonal Activities

Every season brings distinct opportunities to connect with nature across Michigan's varied landscapes:

  1. Kayaking and Paddleboarding: Gliding across crystal-clear inland lakes during warm summer afternoons.
  2. Backpacking and Camping: Sleeping under dark starry skies along secluded forest paths.
  3. Autumn Color Tours: Driving through scenic tree-lined tunnels as maples turn vivid shades of red and gold.
  4. Snowshoeing and Cross-Country Skiing: Exploring silent, snow-covered trails throughout winter.

Essential Packing Checklist for Day Hikers

Proper preparation ensures a safe and enjoyable journey on the trails. Always carry these essential items:

  • Reusable insulated water bottle (at least 2 liters)
  • Topographic trail map and reliable magnetic compass
  • Compact first-aid kit with blister prevention bandages
  • Lightweight waterproof rain shell jacket
  • High-protein trail mix, dried fruit, and electrolyte packets
  • Sun protection: polarized sunglasses, broad-spectrum sunscreen, and brimmed hat
